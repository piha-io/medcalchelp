# Dimensional Analysis: The Foundation of Medical Math

Dimensional analysis is the single most important skill for medical calculations. This systematic approach eliminates guesswork and ensures accuracy in every calculation.

## What is Dimensional Analysis?

Dimensional analysis (also called the factor-label method) is a problem-solving technique that uses conversion factors to move from one unit to another. It's like building a bridge from what you have to what you need.

### The Basic Principle

Every conversion factor equals 1:
- 1000 mg = 1 g, so (1000 mg/1 g) = 1
- 60 minutes = 1 hour, so (60 min/1 hr) = 1

Multiplying by 1 doesn't change the value, only the units!

## Setting Up Dimensional Analysis

### Step 1: Identify What You Have and What You Need

**Example**: Convert 2.5 grams to milligrams
- **Have**: 2.5 g
- **Need**: ? mg

### Step 2: Set Up the Conversion

Write your starting value and multiply by a conversion factor:

```
2.5 g × (1000 mg/1 g) = ?
```

### Step 3: Cancel Units

Units cancel like fractions:

```
2.5 g × (1000 mg/1 g) = 2,500 mg
       ↑_______↑
       These cancel
```

### Step 4: Calculate

2.5 × 1000 = 2,500 mg

## Essential Conversion Factors

### Weight/Mass
- 1 kg = 1000 g
- 1 g = 1000 mg
- 1 mg = 1000 mcg
- 1 kg = 2.2 lb

### Volume
- 1 L = 1000 mL
- 1 mL = 1 cc
- 1 tsp = 5 mL
- 1 tbsp = 15 mL
- 1 oz = 30 mL

### Time
- 1 hr = 60 min
- 1 min = 60 sec
- 1 day = 24 hr

## Multi-Step Conversions

Sometimes you need multiple conversion factors. Line them up so units cancel:

**Example**: Convert 5 mg/kg/min to mg/hr for an 80 kg patient

```
5 mg     80 kg    60 min
------ × ----- × ------- = ?
kg·min     1       1 hr

= 5 × 80 × 60 mg/hr = 24,000 mg/hr
```

## Medication Calculation Examples

### Example 1: Tablet Dosing
**Order**: 500 mg
**Available**: 250 mg tablets

```
500 mg × (1 tablet/250 mg) = 2 tablets
```

### Example 2: Liquid Medication
**Order**: 40 mg
**Available**: 10 mg/5 mL

```
40 mg × (5 mL/10 mg) = 20 mL
```

### Example 3: IV Flow Rate
**Order**: 1000 mL over 8 hours
**Find**: mL/hr

```
1000 mL × (1/8 hr) = 125 mL/hr
```

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### 1. Flipping Conversion Factors
Always arrange so unwanted units cancel:
- ✓ Right: 500 mg × (1 g/1000 mg) = 0.5 g
- ✗ Wrong: 500 mg × (1000 mg/1 g) = 500,000 mg²/g

### 2. Forgetting All Units
Include every unit in your setup:
- ✓ Right: 5 mg/kg/min
- ✗ Wrong: 5 mg/kg (missing time unit)

### 3. Not Checking Final Units
Your answer should have the units you need:
- Question asks for mL/hr? Answer must be in mL/hr
- Question asks for tablets? Answer must be in tablets

## Practice Strategy

### Start Simple
Begin with single-step conversions:
1. g → mg
2. L → mL
3. hr → min

### Build Complexity
Move to multi-step problems:
1. mg/kg → total mg
2. mg/hr → mL/hr
3. units/kg/hr → mL/hr

### Always Show Work
Write every step:
1. Starting value
2. Each conversion factor
3. Unit cancellation
4. Final calculation

## The Power of Dimensional Analysis

This method works for ANY conversion:
- Simple unit changes
- Complex medication calculations
- IV flow rates
- Dosage adjustments

Master this technique, and you'll never be stumped by a medical math problem again!
