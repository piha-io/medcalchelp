# Complete Guide to Insulin Dosing Calculations

Insulin dosing is one of the most critical calculations in healthcare. This comprehensive guide covers everything from basic sliding scales to advanced carbohydrate counting.

## Understanding Insulin Types

Before diving into calculations, it's essential to understand the different types of insulin:

### Rapid-Acting Insulin
- **Onset**: 15 minutes
- **Peak**: 1-2 hours
- **Duration**: 4-6 hours
- **Examples**: Humalog, NovoLog, Apidra

### Short-Acting Insulin
- **Onset**: 30 minutes
- **Peak**: 2-3 hours
- **Duration**: 6-8 hours
- **Example**: Regular insulin

### Intermediate-Acting Insulin
- **Onset**: 2-4 hours
- **Peak**: 4-12 hours
- **Duration**: 12-18 hours
- **Example**: NPH insulin

### Long-Acting Insulin
- **Onset**: 2 hours
- **Peak**: No pronounced peak
- **Duration**: Up to 24 hours
- **Examples**: Lantus, Levemir

## Basic Insulin Calculations

### 1. Total Daily Dose (TDD)

The total daily dose is the starting point for all insulin calculations.

```
TDD = 0.5-0.6 units/kg/day (for Type 1 diabetes)
TDD = 0.7-1.0 units/kg/day (for Type 2 diabetes)
```

**Example**: 
- Patient weight: 70 kg
- Type 1 diabetes
- TDD = 70 kg × 0.5 = 35 units/day

### 2. Basal/Bolus Split

Typically, insulin is divided into:
- **Basal**: 40-50% of TDD
- **Bolus**: 50-60% of TDD

**Example**:
- TDD = 35 units
- Basal = 35 × 0.5 = 17.5 units
- Bolus = 35 × 0.5 = 17.5 units

## Carbohydrate Counting

### Insulin-to-Carbohydrate Ratio (ICR)

The ICR tells you how many grams of carbohydrates 1 unit of insulin covers.

```
ICR = 500 ÷ TDD
```

**Example**:
- TDD = 35 units
- ICR = 500 ÷ 35 = 14.3
- **Result**: 1 unit covers 14 grams of carbs

### Calculating Meal Insulin

```
Meal insulin = Carbs in meal ÷ ICR
```

**Example**:
- Meal contains 60g carbs
- ICR = 1:14
- Insulin needed = 60 ÷ 14 = 4.3 units

## Correction Factor (Sensitivity Factor)

The correction factor tells you how much 1 unit of insulin will lower blood glucose.

### Calculating Correction Factor

```
Correction Factor = 1700 ÷ TDD (for rapid-acting)
Correction Factor = 1500 ÷ TDD (for regular insulin)
```

**Example**:
- TDD = 35 units
- Using rapid-acting insulin
- CF = 1700 ÷ 35 = 48.6
- **Result**: 1 unit drops BG by ~50 mg/dL

### Correction Dose Calculation

```
Correction dose = (Current BG - Target BG) ÷ CF
```

**Example**:
- Current BG = 250 mg/dL
- Target BG = 120 mg/dL
- CF = 50
- Correction = (250 - 120) ÷ 50 = 2.6 units

## Sliding Scale Insulin

Sliding scales provide insulin doses based on blood glucose ranges.

### Sample Sliding Scale

| Blood Glucose (mg/dL) | Insulin Dose |
|----------------------|--------------|
| < 150                | No insulin   |
| 150-200              | 2 units      |
| 201-250              | 4 units      |
| 251-300              | 6 units      |
| 301-350              | 8 units      |
| > 350                | 10 units + call MD |

## Advanced Calculations

### Combined Meal + Correction Dose

```
Total dose = Meal insulin + Correction insulin
```

**Example**:
- Meal: 60g carbs, ICR 1:14 = 4.3 units
- BG: 220, Target: 120, CF: 50 = 2 units
- **Total**: 4.3 + 2 = 6.3 units

### Insulin on Board (IOB)

Consider active insulin to prevent stacking:

```
Adjusted correction = Correction dose - IOB
```

## Safety Considerations

### Critical Safety Points

1. **Always verify calculations** - Double-check all math
2. **Round appropriately** - Usually to nearest 0.5 or whole unit
3. **Consider timing** - Account for insulin onset and peak
4. **Monitor closely** - Check BG frequently after dosing
5. **Document everything** - Record doses and responses

### When to Hold or Reduce Insulin

- NPO status
- Hypoglycemia (BG < 70)
- Decreased oral intake
- Increased physical activity
- Concurrent illness affecting intake

## Practice Problems

### Problem 1: Calculate TDD and ICR
- Patient: 80 kg, Type 1 diabetes
- Calculate TDD and insulin-to-carb ratio

**Solution**:
- TDD = 80 × 0.5 = 40 units
- ICR = 500 ÷ 40 = 12.5 (1:12 ratio)

### Problem 2: Meal + Correction
- Current BG: 280 mg/dL
- Target BG: 130 mg/dL
- Meal: 75g carbs
- ICR: 1:15, CF: 40

**Solution**:
- Meal insulin = 75 ÷ 15 = 5 units
- Correction = (280 - 130) ÷ 40 = 3.75 units
- Total = 5 + 3.75 = 8.75 units (round to 9)

## Key Takeaways

1. **Start with TDD** - All calculations flow from total daily dose
2. **Know your ratios** - ICR and CF are patient-specific
3. **Consider the whole picture** - Timing, activity, illness all matter
4. **Safety first** - When in doubt, give less and recheck
5. **Document and learn** - Track what works for each patient

Remember: These are general guidelines. Always follow your institution's protocols and physician orders. Individual patient needs vary significantly.
